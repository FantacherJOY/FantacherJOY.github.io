Current

  • Digital Twin Framework (DINIRS)

    Causal inference · Digital twin

    Choosing between noninvasive respiratory support (NIRS) and invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV) for patients with acute respiratory failure is a complex, time-sensitive decision with heterogeneous treatment effects across patient subgroups. We developed and validated a censoring-aware Digital Twin framework for Individualized Treatment Effects of Non-Invasive Respiratory Support (DINIRS) to estimate individualized treatment effects (ITEs) that capture mortality and ventilation duration.

  • NIRS Ontology for Acute Care

    Biomedical ontology

    In this study, we introduced NIRS ontology to support knowledge representation in acute care settings by providing a unified framework that enhances data clarity and interoperability, laying the groundwork for future clinical decision-making research. The ontology has 145 classes, 11 object properties, and 18 data properties across 949 axioms that establish concept relationships.

    DOI · BioPortal · GitHub

    Nov 2024 – Jul 2025 · OWL, Protégé, MIMIC-IV

  • ICU Liberation Bundle Standardization

    Health informatics

    This study aims to characterize data elements of the A-F liberation bundle using a large, single-center critical care database and to develop standardized bundle cards that map bundle components to controlled vocabularies. We developed and validated six ICU Liberation Bundle cards that map bundle components to standardized vocabularies and CDMs, enabling retrospective adherence evaluation in real-world data.

  • Fungal Infections in the All of Us Research Program

    Clinical informatics

    Fungal infections, such as coccidioidomycosis, aspergillosis, and histoplasmosis, represent a growing public health concern in the United States. We constructed three cohorts, coccidioidomycosis (n = 1,171), aspergillosis (n = 687), and histoplasmosis (n = 347), among 393,601 participants with electronic health record data.

  • Distributed Generation with Wind Turbines & Smart Contracts

    Energy systems

    This study develops and solves three mathematical optimization models to determine wind turbines' optimal siting and sizing in distributed generation (DG) systems operating in industrial parks or residential house settings that may connect to a substation or operate in an islanded mode. The third model integrates a smart contract mechanism into the second model to also ensure transparent, secure, and efficient energy transactions between the factories (or houses).

    1. Model 1stochastic integer non-linear program
    2. Model 2energy storage systems (ESSs), bi-directional energy trading
    3. Model 3smart contract mechanism

    tested in six US cities
